# Falam Linguistic Guidelines

## 1. Purpose

This document defines rules for maintaining linguistic integrity in the Falam dictionary.

## 2. Falam Identity

The dictionary is specifically concerned with Falam Chin.

Do not assume that a word from another Chin language is Falam simply because it looks, sounds, or means something similar.

Language identification must come from reliable evidence.

## 3. Spelling

Preserve the spelling used by the authoritative source.

Do not silently correct uncommon spellings.

If multiple spellings are genuinely used, document their relationship rather than arbitrarily deleting one.

## 4. Unicode

The application must correctly support:

- Falam characters
- diacritics where applicable
- Unicode normalization
- punctuation
- apostrophes
- special characters

Never assume ASCII-only text.

## 5. Dialects and Variants

If regional or dialect variation exists, represent it explicitly.

Do not merge different forms merely because they have the same English meaning.

## 6. Part of Speech

Use standardized categories where reliable information exists.

Examples:

- noun
- verb
- adjective
- adverb
- pronoun
- preposition
- conjunction
- interjection
- particle
- phrase

Do not guess a part of speech.

## 7. Meanings

English definitions should represent the actual meaning in context.

If a Falam word has several distinct senses, represent them separately.

## 8. Examples

Examples should preferably come from:

- authoritative sources
- native speakers
- verified linguistic material

AI-generated examples must be treated as unverified until reviewed.

## 9. Pronunciation

Pronunciation information must be evidence-based.

For native audio:

- record the exact intended word
- preserve natural pronunciation
- identify the speaker internally
- verify the recording against the dictionary entry
- retain multiple legitimate pronunciations when appropriate

## 10. Native Speaker Review

A knowledgeable native speaker should be involved in verification whenever possible.

Recommended process:

Source Data → Data Processing → Native Speaker Review → Corrections → Verification

## 11. AI Restrictions

AI can assist with organization and processing.

AI must not be treated as the linguistic authority.

Verified source > AI output.

## 12. Uncertainty

When information is uncertain:

Do not guess. Flag for review.
